> **Protocol deviation:** the brief named the model families (circle with equant, Kepler ellipse) and Kepler's 8′. This run is therefore a blind measurement under prescribed models, not a blind discovery. A model-agnostic re-run is in `../agnostic-fit/` (in progress).

Generated by `report.py` from `results.json` (both produced by `python fit.py`). Protocol: `PLAN.md` (sections 1–8 plus Amendment A, all written before any fit). Units: Earth's a; frame: mean ecliptic and equinox J1590.0; epoch J1590.0 = JD(TT) 2301792.5. All residuals are observed − model in arcmin; longitude residuals are multiplied by cos b.

**Blindness statement.** I know roughly what the modern orbits of Mars and Earth are. That knowledge is not used in any starting value, bound, prior or check. Starting values came from the data: 8 oppositions from the retrograde arcs (robust cubic through the retrograde rows); mean-motion aliases from the opposition longitudes (0.0642, 0.5249, 0.9856, 1.4464 … °/d); synodic period 781.34 d; two-circle fits of each alias pair chose P_M ≈ 689 d and P_E ≈ 368 d (next-best pair 1.37× the cost); node from the ascending latitude zero-crossings (heliocentric 40.7, 38.5, 37.2, 124.9°; circular median used, the 125° crossing is an outlier); inclination from a latitude-only fit. Light time and annual aberration are modelled from each model's own orbits, with Earth's a = 499.005 light-seconds (the only external constant). Precession self-test: 50.21"/yr.

## Kepler's 8′ question

**Answer: YES.** By the pre-registered rule, model 1c (circle + free equant, same Kepler Earth as model 3) leaves a systematic longitude error of 17.0′; model 3 leaves 1.55′. Model 1, with a circular Earth, leaves 52.56′. The ellipse removes the error with either the free or the bisected equant.

Kepler-style test (Earth fixed to model 3; the equant's longitude parameters fitted to near-opposition rows only, then R, Ω and i to all of model 3's clean rows). It uses 64 rows within 30° of opposition, not the moments of opposition Kepler used, so it is not a reconstruction of his construction:
- Free equant: near opposition RMS 8.133′ (median 4.118′); elsewhere RMS 16.898′, up to 62.34′.
- Bisected equant: near opposition RMS 10.731′; elsewhere RMS 8.756′, up to 27.21′.

**Post hoc (added after seeing the residuals).** Geocentric residuals near opposition were scaled by Δ/r (model 3 geometry), which turns them into heliocentric-equivalent errors, the quantity Kepler compared:
- model 3: RMS 0.5′, worst bin 0.66′;
- free equant (1c): RMS 3.32′, worst bin 5.04′;
- bisected equant (1cb): RMS 3.93′, worst bin 6.54′.

Fitted to the whole record (185 rows after its own 5σ rule) with the same Keplerian Earth as the ellipse model, a circle with a free equant leaves systematic geocentric longitude errors of up to 17.0′ (second-harmonic amplitude 8.26′); the ellipse reduces them to 1.55′ (0.37′), the measurement noise. Scaled to heliocentric longitude near opposition, the equant's error is 3–6′ at the octants, of the order of the 8′ Kepler reported. The test was pre-registered before the fit; the heliocentric scaling was added after seeing the residuals and is marked post hoc. We did not repeat Kepler's own construction (a vicarious hypothesis fitted to four acronychal longitudes), so we do not claim to have recovered his 8′. Seen from Earth near opposition, the heliocentric-equivalent error is amplified about 2.5–3×.

## Disclosures

- **Inclination.** The fitted i of Mars is 13.7″ (3.0σ) above what every modern comparator gives (DE441 osculating, span-mean, and the same model fitted to DE441 positions at the same nights). It is a reduction-level zero point (the data's mean latitude is +0.26′ against DE441; star catalogue, instrument zeros and refraction are the likely sources), not a property of the model and not an agreement with modern values. The formal σ do not contain it.
- **Period against osculating elements.** If P is compared with the osculating J1590.0 value, note that the osculating period varies by ±0.038 d over the span (Jupiter and Earth pull on Mars); the difference is one of definition, not an error.
- **Pre-registration evidence.** `PLAN.md`, `fit.py` and `ORBIT.json` entered the repository in one commit. That the plan was written before the fit rests on the plan's own statement and `run.log`, not on git history.
- **Horizons on the reduction side.** The reduction of every session was verified against JPL Horizons before the fit; the quality-class flag lists were revised once after seeing a class-level residual table (no per-session residual); five suspect sessions were listed, from Horizons, before the fit and left in. Horizons did not enter any position. See `../out/BATCH.md`.
- **Modern reference stars.** Modern Hipparcos positions of the reference stars (not of the planet) were used in the reduction.

## Notes and deviations

- The Huber stage (f_scale = 2) is solved by iteratively reweighted least squares. scipy's built-in Huber loss did not converge here, and the estimator is the same.
- The Kepler-style test uses model 3's clean rows (implementation choice, not in PLAN.md).
- Post hoc: the Δ/r analysis and its figure, and `post_hoc.curves` in results.json. That curve (model-vs-model heliocentric longitude) also absorbs each model's different Earth, so it is not used for the answer.
- Model 3's χ²_red of 2.454 means the formal per-row σ are about 1.6× too small. The quoted σ include this, and the bootstrap agrees.
- Diurnal parallax and refraction are not modelled (the positions are given as geocentric).
