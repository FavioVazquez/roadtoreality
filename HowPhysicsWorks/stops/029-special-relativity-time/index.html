Special Relativity: Time Dilation

Moving clocks run slow

1905 · Albert Einstein

Einstein showed that a moving clock, seen from the frame it moves through, runs slow — and this is not an illusion. GPS satellites require relativity corrections to stay accurate.

The Twin Paradox

Imagine twins. One stays on Earth; the other boards a rocket. When the traveling twin returns, they are younger — not metaphorically, but physically. Their heart has beaten fewer times. Their cells have divided fewer times. This is time dilation: moving clocks run slow. The simulation shows the outbound leg as seen from Earth. Seen from the rocket, it is Earth's clock that runs slow; the asymmetry that makes the traveller younger at the reunion comes from turning around, which changes the rocket's frame and not Earth's.

The Lorentz Factor γ

The ratio of the twins’ aging rates is the Lorentz factor γ. At everyday speeds γ is indistinguishable from 1. At 99% the speed of light, γ ≈ 7 — the traveling twin ages at one seventh the rate of the Earth twin, as judged from Earth.

It Is Not an Illusion

Time dilation has been measured directly. In 1972 Hafele and Keating flew atomic clocks around the world. Flown eastward, with Earth's rotation, the clocks came back 59 nanoseconds behind the ground clocks; flown westward they came back 273 nanoseconds ahead, exactly as relativity, counting both speed and altitude, predicted.[2] Fast muons last longer too: in the CERN storage ring, muons at γ = 29.3 lived as dilation predicts, to 0.1 %.[4] GPS satellites, travelling at about 3.9 km/s, carry clocks that would run slow by about 7 microseconds a day from their speed alone. They also sit in weaker gravity than we do, which (general relativity, stop 034) makes them run fast by about 45 microseconds a day. The net effect, about 38 microseconds a day fast, is built into the system; left uncorrected it would grow into a position error of about 10 km per day.[5]

Key Concept
$$\Delta t = \gamma \, \Delta t_0$$
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$

At $v = 0.99c$, $\gamma \approx 7.09$ — the moving twin ages at one seventh the rate, as judged from Earth.
