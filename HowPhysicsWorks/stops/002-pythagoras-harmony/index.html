Pythagoras and Mathematical Harmony

Music, numbers, and the shape of the cosmos

~500 BCE · Pythagoras

The question

Which ratios of string length give the octave, the fifth and the fourth, and how does shortening a string change its pitch?

The Pythagoreans are credited with finding that the consonant intervals correspond to simple ratios of string lengths: 2:1 the octave, 3:2 the fifth, 4:3 the fourth. The story that Pythagoras heard it in a blacksmith's hammers (told by Nicomachus six centuries later) cannot be true as told: a hammer's pitch does not follow its weight that way. The monochord experiment is real; who first did it is not known. It is the earliest Greek case we know of in which a sensation (consonance) was tied to a number; Babylonian astronomers had been fitting the sky with arithmetic for centuries.

Click a ratio button to hear both strings together and see the second string's length

What to try

  1. Press 2:1 Octave: you hear both tones together, the second string is drawn half as long, and its label reads f = 220 Hz × 2/1 = 440 Hz.
  2. Press Play, then 3:2 Fifth: the second string, length L × 2/3, swings three times for every two swings of the reference string.
  3. Press 4:3 Fourth: the second string is L × 3/4 and sounds 293.3 Hz. Shorter string, higher pitch, in exact inverse proportion.
  4. Press 9:8 Whole Tone: the string is L × 8/9 at 247.5 Hz. The ratio needs larger numbers, and it is not one of the three consonances, 2:1, 3:2 and 4:3.

How to explore

  • → Click any ratio button to hear Pythagoras's intervals — the browser plays two sine tones together: 220 Hz for the reference string and 220 Hz × ratio for the second
  • → Watch the second string: a string 2/3 as long vibrates 3/2 as fast. Frequency is inversely proportional to length; that is the whole experiment.
  • → The fixed end points show where each string never moves; both strings sound their fundamental, one half-wave between the ends

What the Simulation Leaves Out

Each button plays two pure sine tones, 220 Hz and 220 Hz × the ratio, fading out in under a second; a real plucked string also sounds overtones above its fundamental. Frequency follows 1/length only for strings of the same material under the same tension, which the simulation takes for granted. The drawn strings swing far more slowly than the tones you hear: only the ratio of their two swing rates is true.

Key Concept
  • Mathematics is not just a tool for describing nature — Pythagoras argued it is the very substance of nature. Every physical law we have discovered since is written in mathematics.
  • $$\frac{f_2}{f_1} = \frac{L_1}{L_2}$$ (shorter string, higher pitch: frequency is inversely proportional to length)
