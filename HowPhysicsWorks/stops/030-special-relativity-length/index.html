Special Relativity: Length Contraction

Moving objects measure shorter

1905 · Albert Einstein

The question

How long does a 100 m train car measure from the platform as its speed nears light speed, and which of its dimensions shrink?

Objects moving at high speed are measured as shorter along their direction of motion. The effect is mutual: the passenger measures our rulers as the short ones.

Moving Objects Measure Shorter

A meter stick moving past you at relativistic speed is measured as shorter along its direction of motion. At 99% the speed of light, it measures only about 14 cm — not because anything happened to the stick, but because of how lengths are compared between frames.

Only Along the Direction of Motion

Length contraction is strictly along the direction of travel. The height and width perpendicular to motion are unchanged. A sphere moving at 0.99c is measured as an ellipsoid flattened to a seventh of its width along the motion. A photograph would show something else: light from its far side takes longer to arrive, and for a sphere that effect exactly cancels the flattening, so the outline stays circular and the sphere appears rotated instead (the Terrell–Penrose effect, 1959).[2][3] The simulation shows the measured shape, not a photograph.

The Same Root Cause as Time Dilation

Time dilation and length contraction are two sides of the same spacetime geometry. They are both required to keep the speed of light constant for all inertial observers. You cannot have one without the other. The third piece, and the one that resolves every apparent paradox, is that observers in relative motion disagree about which events are simultaneous. Measuring a length means marking both ends at the same instant, and ‘the same instant’ is frame-dependent. That is where the contraction comes from.

What the Simulation Leaves Out

Only the platform's measurement is drawn; the passenger's matching view, in which the platform's rulers are the short ones, is not. The wheels stay round and keep their size as the car shortens, a drawing shortcut: measured from the platform they too would be flattened along the motion. The car's drift across the screen is a slow loop whose speed is not to scale, and L₀ = 100 m is an example length.

Key Concept
$$L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma} = L_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$

At $v = 0.99c$, a 100-meter ruler is measured as only $\approx 14.1$ meters long.
