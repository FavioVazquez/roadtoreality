Hertz and Radio Waves

Maxwell's invisible light, detected at last

1887 · Heinrich Hertz

The question

In which directions does an oscillating dipole radiate, and how does its frequency set the spacing of the wavefronts?

In 1887 Heinrich Hertz built a spark-gap transmitter and a loop detector a few metres away and saw sparks answer sparks; in 1888 he set up standing waves against a metal sheet, measured their wavelength, and showed the waves travel at the speed of light and reflect, refract and polarise like light, as Maxwell had predicted twenty-three years earlier.

What to try

  1. Press Reset, then Play, and compare directions: the rings are brightest to the left and right of the dipole and fade to nothing straight above and below it, along the antenna axis.
  2. Raise Dipole frequency from 1.2 to 3.0: the readout λ drops from 83 px to 33 px and new rings come out faster and closer together.
  3. Set Dipole frequency to 1.0 and Animation speed (px/s) to 200: λ reads 200 px, the animation speed divided by the frequency. Rings of the same colour are one λ apart; a red ring and the next blue ring are half a λ apart.

A dipole antenna — two conducting rods with alternating charge — is the simplest EM radiator. The radiation is not uniform in all directions: it is zero directly along the antenna axis and strongest perpendicular to it. This sin²θ pattern is why a radio antenna is mounted vertically for horizontal coverage, and why Hertz saw nothing when his loop faced the ends of his dipole.

Each half-cycle of oscillation reverses the E-field direction, producing a new wavefront of opposite polarity. The colored rings in the simulation show successive half-cycles: red for positive E-field, blue for negative. Rings of the same colour are one wavelength λ = v/f apart; a red ring and the next blue ring are half a wavelength apart.

What the Simulation Leaves Out

The rings show the sin²θ pattern as brightness only; they do not dim as they spread, and the field close to the antenna is not drawn. The wave speed is in pixels per second, not c, and time advances a fixed 0.016 s per frame, so the frequency in Hz is in animation time. The charges sliding ±20 px along the antenna are a picture of the oscillating dipole, not drawn to scale against the wavelength, and the scene is a flat slice through the antenna.

Key Concepts
  • Hertz proved that oscillating electric charges radiate electromagnetic waves — exactly as Maxwell's equations predicted. The radiation pattern is not spherical: it is zero along the dipole axis and maximum perpendicular to it.
  • $$I(\theta) \propto \sin^2\!\theta$$
  • The emitted wave travels at the speed of light. Each half-cycle reverses the E-field direction — the alternating red and blue rings represent successive half-cycles.
  • $$c = \lambda f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}}$$
  • Hertz's experiments of 1887–88 directly confirmed Maxwell's 1865 prediction and opened the door to radio, television, and wireless communication.
